\subsection*{Draft abstract}
We study prediction--retention trade-offs in task-free class-incremental
network intrusion detection with a benign/attack gate and an attack-family
classifier. The comparison separates gate knowledge distillation from
attack-side replay, distillation, and weight alignment. We retain all
evaluated variants: KD+WA, attack-side retention without gate distillation
(AR), and attack-only gate distillation (AKD). The fixed KD+WA candidate
failed the original CSE18 acceptance criteria: reduced forgetting did not
compensate for lower final balanced accuracy than the same-data single
classifier and the original two-stage model. Subsequent AR and AKD designs
were introduced after observing those results and must be identified as
post-unsealing interventions. Development comparisons and released-seed
CSE18 exploration motivate a dataset-dependent retention--plasticity
hypothesis, not an established causal explanation or a universal improvement.
The new-seed decision remains pending. This bounded study addresses RQ2;
silent explanation drift (RQ1), non-suppressive explanation governance
(RQ3), and the project's substantive-innovation requirement remain unresolved.

\subsection*{Draft contribution statement}
The contribution of this bounded study is an evidence-traceable comparison
of where retention is applied within a two-stage incremental classifier,
reporting predictive performance, signed forgetting, Normal/attack safety,
and physical execution costs. KD, DER++, and weight alignment are borrowed
components, not claimed component-level innovations. Shared data exposure
does not imply equal computation: the benign stream, calibration storage,
teacher forwards, and two-network training must remain explicit.
The comparison includes unsuccessful variants and the original failed
decision rather than selecting a successful narrative retrospectively.
Neither engineering integration nor a performance gain alone establishes
the locked substantive-innovation claim.

\subsection*{Draft conclusion}
The available evidence does not establish a single dominant retention
configuration across datasets and operating points. The original fixed
KD+WA candidate failed the CSE18 decision and that result is unchanged.
The Section58 lead report for the five already released CSE18 seeds
describes lower AR forgetting and threshold-dependent accuracy relative
to Replay-BS; these are exploratory observations, not an independent
confirmation. The reported non-significant final-accuracy comparison at
1\% does not establish equivalence or a tie. Claims of superiority over
every comparator, minimum forgetting, or significant development gains
require the corresponding complete, source-bound comparisons before
inclusion in the final abstract.

The mechanism interpretation also remains a hypothesis. In the original
KD+WA implementation, current-task new attacks already receive binary
cross-entropy without gate distillation. Thus any newest-attack deficit
cannot be described as direct distillation of those rows into the Normal
class. AKD instead removes benign rows from the gate-distillation mask
while retaining distillation on replayed old attacks; it tests a different,
indirect explanation of interference. Its results must be reported
alongside KD+WA and AR regardless of outcome.
