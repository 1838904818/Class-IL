\begin{abstract}
Class-incremental network intrusion detection must retain old attacks while learning new ones. Under explicit asymmetric data availability, AR-Stateless retrains a standard histogram-based gradient-boosted learner using refreshed benign data and attack-only memory. Five paired confirmation seeds yield final balanced accuracy of 65.4\% on UNSW-NB15 and 98.3\% on CIC-IDS-2017 at a 1\% calibration false-positive-rate target, compared with ER-Bal-TabT's 56.3\% and 92.7\%. Signed forgetting is lower against this comparator in this view, but not uniformly across methods or decision views. On the previously exposed CSE-CIC-IDS2018 dataset, final balanced accuracy is 81.7\%; the 2.1-point margin over the same-learner literature-memory control is narrow (paired one-sided t $p=0.0491$, exact sign-flip $p=0.0625$), and forgetting is higher than both that control and neural AR. Calibration targets are not guaranteed test false-positive rates. Historical neural-candidate failures remain part of the design-space evidence, and added published-method comparisons are incomplete. Standard learners, replay and calibration are not claimed as novel. These bounded prediction/retention results do not establish explanation stability, governance efficacy or completion of the substantive-innovation requirement.
In the five-seed default-decision comparison, AR exceeds ER-Bal-TabT on UNSW by 1.6 points in accuracy and 2.7 in macro-F1, but trails on CIC by 3.4 and 14.8 points. Under the low-FPR protocol AR trails ER on both datasets; there is no overall winner. These exploratory differences do not override the two-sided exact sign-flip sensitivity result ($p\geq0.0625$ with five pairs).
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} continual learning; intrusion detection; benign data availability; false-positive calibration; replay memory.

\section{Introduction}
Intrusion detection models must recognize newly observed attack families without discarding knowledge of older ones. In class-incremental learning, historical attack records are represented by a limited replay memory, and inference must choose among all observed classes without a task identifier. This constraint is operationally meaningful when previous attack captures cannot be retained indefinitely or reacquired. It does not necessarily imply that benign traffic has the same availability: a monitored network may continue to provide benign examples.

The asymmetry is useful only under an explicit assumption. The learner must have access to a sufficiently clean, representative benign source, distinct from its test data. This paper does not assume that arbitrary production traffic is correctly labeled benign. Nor do we assume that a training-domain benign holdout guarantees the same false-positive rate (FPR) after a distribution change. Both qualifications matter in our results.

Replay-based NIDS research includes class balancing, frequency-aware replacement, representative selection, and other memory designs \citep{cbrs,ecbrs,ssf,acil}. Some multiclass formulations place Normal and attack classes in a common classifier and replay budget. However, this is not a universal description of the literature: binary and novelty-detection methods use different protocols, and prior domain-adaptation work has already used new benign data outside an old attack memory \citep{cerasuolo_rtsi}. The question is therefore not whether benign data have ever been treated differently. It is whether a continuously refreshed, independently calibrated detector provides useful retention and detection behavior in a multistage class-incremental experiment.

We study learning under asymmetric data availability. The selected AR-Stateless candidate fits a fresh multiclass tree learner at each task using newly sampled benign data, current attacks and retained old attacks. A disjoint benign-only calibration pool supplies the optional low-FPR operating point without test labels. The preceding AA-CIL design used a binary gate $G$ and a separate attack-family classifier $F$; it and later neural retention variants are retained as historical design-space controls, not conflated with the selected stateless learner.

The contribution is an evidence-bounded experimental study:
\begin{itemize}
\item An explicit separation of persistent attack replay, refreshed benign training data, and permanent benign calibration data, with each storage and training cost disclosed.
\item A modular gate/family design and matched-protocol comparisons that distinguish restricted benign refresh from attack-memory reallocation.
\item A low-false-alarm evaluation reporting actual test FPR, binary attack detection, family-level performance, and negative registered outcomes rather than relying on aggregate accuracy alone.
\item A separately identified post-hoc control, Replay-BS, demonstrating a dataset-dependent difference under matched benign exposure while retaining the limitation of unmatched total computation.
\item A negative same-dataset AR follow-up: lower forgetting does not establish final-accuracy superiority over Replay-BS, and reduced mean incremental BAcc exposes a plasticity cost. This is a comparative RQ2 finding rather than a new-component or causal-mechanism claim; the lead-reported final summary and its provenance are explicitly distinguished from independently generated earlier results.
\end{itemize}
The study follows the original superiority protocol, the validation-selected retention protocol, and the fixed-KD reassessment, followed by a fixed-capacity retention extension. Their criteria and chronology are given in Appendix~\ref{app:protocols}; each retains its own outcome.

\section{Literature Review}
\subsection{Continual and class-incremental NIDS}
Table~\ref{tab:literature-nids} separates learning settings, benign-data roles, and reporting evidence among the verified papers we reviewed. The six-paper reporting sample includes binary domain-incremental learning and unsupervised novelty detection as well as multiclass Class-IL; it is neither a homogeneous benchmark nor an exhaustive survey.

CBRS-NIDS distinguishes balancing benign versus pooled attacks from balancing original classes \citep{cbrs}. ECBRS uses frequency-driven replacement and minority protection, whereas SSF selects and removes samples according to distributional relevance \citep{ecbrs,ssf}. These are predefined \emph{rules}, not numerically fixed class quotas. SPIDER retains previous-task unlabeled samples and uses gradient-projection memory; CND-IDS instead regularizes latent representations and retains a clean Normal reference set \citep{spider,cndids}. ACIL evaluates multiple replay strategies and explicitly reports forgetting \citep{acil}. ROC-AUC, benign PR-AUC, and Normal-class accuracy must not be substituted for a separately specified benign-FPR operating point.

The closest availability precedents prevent a universal memory-competition claim. Cerasuolo et al.'s Computer Networks model retains old benign and attack exemplars, with 25 exemplars per class rather than a demonstrated constant total budget \citep{cerasuolo_comnet}. Their EAAI study also receives target-domain benign data \citep{cerasuolo_eaai}. Most importantly, their RTSI adaptation adds benign data outside an old attack-only memory \citep{cerasuolo_rtsi}. That experiment already establishes this broad availability pattern; it does not establish repeated benign refresh in an independently trained and calibrated gate over multiple attack increments. TS-mem here is a controlled restricted-refresh ablation, not a faithful reproduction of those systems.

Wang and Cao describe anti-forgetting learning followed by unbiased prediction \citep{wangcao}. Only the official abstract and publication record were available: benign availability, replay contents, and a gate/family interpretation remain unverified. This paper is excluded from the reporting count, and missing full text is not novelty evidence.

Ariffin et al.\ \citep{ariffin2026replayids} retain benign examples throughout replay, sharing our concern for continued benign exposure. Their single TabTransformer uses a shared buffer, without a separate gate or fixed-FPR calibration. They report accuracy, macro-F1, forgetting and intransigence on eight CICIDS2017 classes with seed 42, but no FPR. Their Section~5.7 reports replay final overall accuracy of 0.964/0.966/0.948 for MLP/CNN/FT-Transformer under a separate preprocessing pipeline; these are not balanced accuracies or matched comparisons with ours. Our registered backbone/capacity checks remain pending; no comparative advantage is established. This additional context does not change the six-paper sample below.

\paragraph{G1: Joint operating-point and retention reporting.}
Five of the six papers in the targeted reporting sample do not separately report fixed-operating-point benign FPR in their inspected main results; ACIL has partial or indirect benign reporting. The review record does not establish a joint forgetting-and-fixed-FPR detection evaluation in this sample. This bounded finding is not a claim that no prior paper reports such evidence, nor that unverified forgetting fields mean absence.
\paragraph{G2: Conditional competition for benign replay.}
Shared benign/attack memory in CBRS, ECBRS, SSF, ACIL, and the Computer Networks study motivates examining limited replay allocation. Competition under a fixed total budget is a setting-dependent constraint, not a universal property or an identified cause of forgetting. RTSI and CND-IDS are explicit counterexamples to a universal shared-buffer description. The unresolved empirical question is the incremental value of repeated benign refresh and separate family learning under disclosed storage and exposure costs.

\subsection{Bias correction in Class-IL}
BiC learns a post-training correction \citep{bic}; weight alignment adjusts classifier scale \citep{wa}; separated softmax and taskwise distillation address cross-task interference \citep{ssil}; end-to-end incremental learning includes balanced fine-tuning \citep{eeil}. Logit adjustment uses class-prior information to alter scores and is a long-tail classification approach, not by itself a Class-IL algorithm \citep{menon}. These are relevant controls for shared-classifier imbalance, but their distinct training, calibration, and inference operations must not be conflated.

\paragraph{G3: From class bias to benign-alert control.}
These reviewed formulations do not themselves specify the refreshed benign source, independent benign--attack gate, and benign-only FPR calibration studied here. Correcting old/new-class bias therefore does not establish control of benign-to-attack false alarms or preservation of attack-family recall. This is a protocol and endpoint distinction, not a proof that the original methods cannot be adapted, or that a binary gate is novel. Negative results for our tabular adaptations do not reject the original algorithms in general.

\subsection{Replay and distillation}
iCaRL combines exemplar retention and incremental representation learning \citep{icarl}; Dark Experience Replay and DER++ retain historical responses alongside supervised rehearsal \citep{der}. They supply replay and retention references, not ready-made evidence for decoupled NIDS. Their mechanisms and buffering schemes must not be treated as interchangeable. Image-domain results and original buffer policies are not automatically reproduced by tabular adaptations.

Our fixed-capacity KD+WA candidate reuses distillation, DER-style replay, and weight alignment; none of these components is claimed as new. \emph{The remaining G3 evaluation gap} is whether their combination in separate detection and family learners preserves old attacks while learning new ones at a disclosed false-alarm operating point. That claim requires matched controls and joint accuracy/forgetting reporting, not simply the presence of KD or WA. The failed CSE confirmation reported below limits this candidate's claim rather than changing the literature gap. G1--G3 organize this bounded prediction/retention study under the broader RQ2; they do not replace the unanswered explanation-drift RQ1 or governance RQ3.

\section{Problem Analysis}
\subsection{A corrected experimental basis}
The motivating experiments use a taskwise minimum-update schedule rather than only a small fixed number of epochs. This prevents late, rare attack tasks from receiving arbitrarily few optimizer updates. Old attack classes are accessible only through stored training and validation rows after their introduction. Frozen preparation and duplicate/conflict handling are retained across methods; ``deduplicated UNSW-NB15'' does not imply that every benchmark is free of feature collisions or label conflicts. Historical experiments that revisited complete old attack pools are not used as evidence for the present access-constrained protocol. That old-pool resampling was a bug in this project's earlier implementation. The undertraining and data-quality issues identified in that development history are not attributed to published methods without source-specific evidence.

\subsection{False alarms are important, but not a universal root cause}
An earlier decomposition of the historical S2000 DER++ final confusion matrices showed substantial Normal-origin false positives in CIC-IDS-2017 attack-family columns, including families with high recall but low precision. These DER++ proportions must not be assigned to every method. UNSW-NB15 also exhibited attack-to-attack confusions and lost attack recall. These observations justify measuring benign FPR and attack detection separately, but do not identify inadequate benign coverage or Normal--attack memory competition as a universal causal explanation. The decomposition is arithmetic, not an intervention proving a mechanism.

Under the registered correction sweep, the best tested correction/base combinations closed only \BiasClosureUNSW\% and \BiasClosureCIC\% of the positive Joint-minus-incremental Macro-F1 gaps on UNSW-NB15 and CIC-IDS-2017, respectively. This rejects the sufficiency of those specific adaptations and candidate ranges, not bias correction in general. Joint is an offline reference rather than a guaranteed numerical upper bound.

\subsection{Why change the learning organization?}
The preceding FARQ experiments adjusted shared replay allocation using measured validation false alarms. Their registered main criterion failed, and descriptive operating curves did not establish a universally superior false-alarm/detection frontier. Replay+LA and Replay share the same attainable predictions when the total adjustment is recalibrated; this does not make all allocation methods threshold-equivalent. WACB and Freq change the learned model and improve detection relative to Replay at evaluated matched-FPR points on UNSW-NB15 and CIC-IDS-2017. Memory reallocation can therefore improve the measured trade-off, although it does not close every offline-reference gap. These finite operating-point comparisons are not a global frontier theorem. AA-CIL tests a different learning organization, not a premise that every shared-classifier method merely moves along one common curve.

\section{Method: AR-Stateless and historical neural controls}
\subsection{Selected stateless learner}
AR-Stateless (R3-A) uses one standard multiclass histogram-based
gradient-boosted learner, including Normal, rather than the two neural
networks described below. Each task fits a new learner from scratch on the
current new-class training rows, the retained old attack memory, and the
task's benign training sample. No learned model parameters are carried into
the next task. The attack-memory budget is $200K_t$, where $K_t$ includes
Normal; Normal's share is transferred to attacks. Old attack examples cannot
be replenished from their original pools. The benign calibration pool is
permanently excluded from fitting. Default inference chooses the highest
scoring seen class; the low-FPR view separately calibrates the attack-versus-
benign score. R3-L retains the same learner but stores 200 examples of each
seen class, including Normal, without refreshed benign access.

The neural gate/family design, retention objectives and architecture diagram
in the following subsections are historical controls in the design-space
study, not the architecture or optimization procedure of AR-Stateless.
The shared access assumption is stated first; neural-specific update counts,
cross-entropy, distillation and weight alignment do not apply to the tree.
\subsection{Data roles and access assumption}
At update $t$, let $\mathcal A_t$ be the observed attack families and $\mathcal N_t$ the newly introduced attack families. The learner receives their current training data $D_t^A$, retained old attack training memory $R_{t-1}^{A,\mathrm{tr}}$, and a benign training source $B^{\mathrm{tr}}$. A permanent benign calibration pool $B^{\mathrm{cal}}$ is disjoint from every optimizer input. We assume continued access to $B^{\mathrm{tr}}$ but not to the original pools of old attacks.

The benchmark simulates benign availability by drawing a task-dependent sample $B_t\subseteq B^{\mathrm{tr}}$ of up to \GateBenignRows\ rows. Samples may overlap across tasks; this is repeated access to a fixed prepared training source, not evidence from a newly collected live stream. The permanent split prevents a calibration row from later becoming a training row.

\subsection{Independent detector and family learner}
The gate is a binary MLP trained on
\begin{equation}
 D_t^G=B_t\cup D_t^A\cup R_{t-1}^{A,\mathrm{tr}}.
\end{equation}
Its labels are benign versus attack. The family MLP is trained on
\begin{equation}
 D_t^F=D_t^A\cup R_{t-1}^{A,\mathrm{tr}},
\end{equation}
using attack-family labels only. Both use the existing class-balanced cross-entropy implementation and retain learned parameters between updates. Their optimizers and costs are separate. Neither model receives calibration data as optimization input.

For exact implementation fidelity, the family network is initialized with the benchmark's full attack-label output dimension. Training follows the shared replay cross-entropy implementation, while inference restricts the argmax to observed attack families. Thus this experiment assumes a known finite benchmark label universe; it is not a claim about an unbounded, dynamically expanding unknown-label vocabulary.

\subsection{Attack replay allocation}
Let $K_t=|\mathcal A_t|+1$, including Normal, and let $m$ be the common per-observed-class budget. The shared capacity is
\begin{equation}
 M_t=mK_t,\qquad m=\MemoryPerClass.
\end{equation}
AA-CIL divides $M_t$ among observed attacks: $q_{t,c}=\lfloor M_t/|\mathcal A_t|\rfloor$ plus one for the first remainder classes in ascending class-ID order. This is a capacity ceiling, not a promise of equal actual occupancy. Each class allocation contains separate training and validation slots; validation rows do not enter optimization. When a class is new, examples can be selected from its prepared training and validation partitions. Once it is old, the corresponding retained arrays are the only permissible source, even if a later quota exceeds available rows. Unused slots remain unused; old pools are never revisited to fill them.

This allocation transfers the nominal Normal share to attacks. It does not make benign data free in a physical-storage sense: both benign training samples and the permanent calibration pool are disclosed outside $M_t$. The no-transfer ablation tests whether this allocation adds value beyond benign refresh.

\subsection{Calibration and inference}
Let $s_G(x)=z_A(x)-z_B(x)$ be the gate's attack-minus-benign logit margin. For $n=|B^{\mathrm{cal}}|$, sort its margins as $s_{(1)}\leq\cdots\leq s_{(n)}$ and set
\begin{equation}
 k_\alpha=\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor,\qquad
 q_{t,\alpha}=s_{(n-k_\alpha)}.
\end{equation}
The strict decision $s_G(x)>q_{t,\alpha}$ bounds the empirical calibration false-alarm count by $k_\alpha$, including ties. No randomized tie breaking is used. Prediction is
\begin{equation}
 \hat y(x)=\begin{cases}
 \mathrm{Normal},&s_G(x)\leq q_{t,\alpha},\\
 \arg\max_{c\in\mathcal A_t}F_c(x),&s_G(x)>q_{t,\alpha}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This is an empirical training-holdout operating point, not a distribution-free guarantee on test or deployment FPR. Gate false negatives cannot be recovered by the downstream family classifier.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{One AA-CIL update}
\label{alg:aacil}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Current attacks $D_t^A$; retained attack memory $R_{t-1}^A$; benign pools $B^{\mathrm{tr}},B^{\mathrm{cal}}$
\State Sample $B_t$ from $B^{\mathrm{tr}}$ only.
\State Train $G$ on $B_t\cup D_t^A\cup R_{t-1}^{A,\mathrm{tr}}$.
\State Train $F$ on $D_t^A\cup R_{t-1}^{A,\mathrm{tr}}$.
\State Allocate attack capacities $q_{t,c}$ within $M_t$.
\For{each observed attack $c$}
\State Select new-class rows, or subsample retained old rows; keep training and validation disjoint.
\EndFor
\State Calibrate $q_{t,\alpha}$ on $B^{\mathrm{cal}}$; do not update weights.
\State Save models, retained memory, allocation, costs, and operating-point confusion matrices.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Post-hoc retention extension: AA-CIL-KD}
The original AA-CIL gate relies on attack replay without an explicit response-retention loss, and its family learner uses ordinary replay. AA-CIL-KD is a post-hoc extension to that study, with its protocol and success criteria registered before observing any KD results. It adds a gate loss
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_G=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CE},G}
       +\lambda_G\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{MSE},G}.
\end{equation}
For retained old attack training rows, the gate targets are the stored two-logit responses recorded when those rows entered memory. For the current benign sample, targets come from the frozen preceding-task gate. New attack rows have no old-class distillation target, and the first task has no preceding-task gate. Neither calibration rows nor attack validation rows become optimizer inputs.

The attack-only family learner uses a task-boundary DER++-objective adaptation: supervised learning is supplemented by replay cross-entropy and MSE against stored family logits. This is not a claim to reproduce the original DER++ online storage schedule. Gate and family logits are auxiliary records for the same attack rows, not two separately allocated sample memories; their storage and the two training costs must be disclosed. The update budgets, benign refresh, permanent holdout and threshold policy remain those of AA-CIL.

An initial development search used mean stagewise validation balanced accuracy and varied three distillation coefficients. It was superseded before formal KD results by the retention-constrained selection below. Its completed runs remain part of the development cost, but do not enter the new selection. The previously planned noGKD ablation is not one of the five family-model candidates in this revised experiment.

\subsection{Post-hoc parameter isolation: AA-CIL-ISO}
AA-CIL-ISO changes only the family learner relative to AA-CIL-KD: the gate, G-KD objective, benign refresh and calibration remain the same. It uses the existing MLP feature encoder. On the first task containing attacks, the encoder and that task's new family heads train jointly within one family-model update budget. The encoder is then frozen. Each new family $c$ receives its own rank-8 residual adapter and two-logit binary head,
\begin{equation}
 h_c=h+U_cV_ch,\qquad p_c(x)=\operatorname{softmax}(H_ch_c)_1.
\end{equation}
The head learns that family versus the other attacks available in the current task and legal old-attack replay. Multiple new families provide negatives for one another. Normal rows are not used as family-head negatives. After the task, its adapters and heads are frozen permanently; old attack pools are never reopened.

All heads introduced in one task share a single training loop: their class-balanced binary cross-entropies are averaged before each optimizer step. Each new head participates in the task's common $\max(8\text{ epochs},2000\text{ steps})$ schedule, rather than receiving an additional serial budget per head. The first encoder training has no separate pretraining budget. Joint family-loop time and shared steps are recorded once; exclusive per-head time is not inferred from that total. If the first task contains only one attack family, there are no other-attack negatives: this limitation is recorded explicitly, without invented negatives or benign substitution.

After the gate declares an attack, prediction is $\arg\max_{c\in\mathcal A_t}p_c(x)$ over independently normalized positive probabilities. This is head-only inference with no router and no cross-head softmax calibration. It adapts OFRA-style parameter isolation to the present backbone and is evaluated under the validation-selected retention protocol, with descriptive paired comparisons against KD.

\subsection{Retention alternatives and validation-only selection}
The revised experiment compares five family learners under the same G-KD gate: KD, ISO, weight alignment (WA), class-balanced fine-tuning (CBF), and nearest-class-mean prediction (NCM). WA rescales newly introduced classifier weights by the mean old-to-new weight-norm ratio after task training \citep{wa}. CBF adds small-learning-rate updates on balanced retained attack training memory \citep{eeil}. NCM computes class prototypes from retained attack training rows encoded by the current family encoder and predicts by feature-space distance \citep{icarl}. These are adaptations of established mechanisms, not claims of novel versions of the cited algorithms. Their additional time, steps, prototype storage and model storage are reported separately.

Only UNSW-NB15 development seeds $\{5,6\}$ select hyperparameters and the main version. Each candidate uses $\lambda_G\in\{0.5,1,2\}$; KD fixes both family DER coefficients to one. CBF uses $0.1$ times the base learning rate and either one or five fine-tuning epochs. A stratified permanent $10\%$ attack holdout supplies fixed validation cohorts across stages; it is never optimized, is accessible only for this development evaluation, and its storage is disclosed outside the replay budget. The permanent Normal holdout supplies the gate threshold at $\alpha=2\%$ and the Normal validation cohort. This development-only access exception does not reopen old attack training pools or change formal replay access.

For each version, eligible configurations have final validation balanced accuracy at least the original AA-CIL development mean minus three percentage points. The configuration with lowest mean validation forgetting is retained, and the lowest-forgetting eligible version is selected as the candidate main method. All choices are frozen across datasets before evaluation seeds $\{1,2,3,4,42\}$ are used. Every version is reported, not only the selected one. If no configuration is eligible, there is no eligible main method; any configuration retained for descriptive formal reporting is explicitly a fallback, not an eligibility success. Test labels never select the gate threshold, hyperparameters, or main version.

\subsection{Fixed-capacity retention candidates}
A subsequent extension constrains the family learner to a fixed parameter count across tasks, no more than twice the full original family model, at most one extra component, and a single inference model. KD-weak reduces the family DER coefficients; KD+WA and KD+CBF apply alignment or balanced fine-tuning after KD. EMA-KD maintains one slow family copy (decay $0.999$), uses replay-logit MSE with weight one, and predicts only with that copy. DUAL uses two fixed encoders and a joint concatenation head: the stable encoder has the frozen KD coefficient, while the plastic encoder has no distillation loss. The all-attack output capacity is allocated initially; unseen classes remain masked until introduction. There is no taskwise branch expansion.

The fixed-capacity retention protocol uses both UNSW-NB15 and NSL-KDD development seeds $\{5,6\}$ with the fixed attack validation cohorts and permanent Normal holdout. It requires each dataset's validation forgetting to be at most its lowest external-five value plus one percentage point before comparing mean validation final BAcc. Within one point of BAcc, the simpler candidate is preferred. Gate coefficients are $\lambda_G\in\{0.25,0.5\}$; KD-weak searches each DER coefficient in $\{0.25,0.5\}$, and the other KD configurations retain coefficients of one. CBF uses one epoch at $0.1$ times the base learning rate. If no candidate meets both forgetting constraints, the lowest-mean-forgetting version is frozen only as an explicitly ineligible descriptive fallback. All five candidates are reported on formal seeds, with UNSW and NSL still designated development datasets.

\subsection{Metrics and registered inference}
For confusion counts $C_{ij}$ with Normal index $b$, we report
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{FPR}&=\frac{\sum_{j\ne b}C_{bj}}{\sum_j C_{bj}},\\
 \mathrm{ADR}&=\frac{\sum_{i\ne b}\sum_{j\ne b}C_{ij}}{\sum_{i\ne b}\sum_j C_{ij}}.
\end{align}
ADR is binary attack detection, not correct-family recall. Macro-F1 and attack-family macro recall supplement it. The registered family-recall guard includes attack classes meeting the specified minimum test support; excluded rare classes remain visible in per-class evidence. Precision is undefined for a class with no predictions in a seed and is never changed to zero. Its group mean and sample standard deviation use only defined seed values, with the actual $n_{\mathrm{defined}}$ (\texttt{defined\_n}) reported separately from the total seed count. If none are defined, the mean remains undefined; with fewer than two defined values, the sample standard deviation is undefined. Such a seed is not dropped from recall, forgetting, or other defined endpoints.

The primary calibration target is $\alpha=\PrimaryAlpha\%$. Actual test FPR is reported alongside it because equal calibration targets need not yield equal test FPR. The original superiority protocol requires a one-sided paired test against the highest-mean registered comparator in at least two datasets. The comparator is chosen once per dataset and endpoint from whole-seed means, not separately per seed. Other criteria concern Macro-F1 harm, attack-family recall, the CIC gate-refresh ablation, and descriptive cost/Joint reporting. Paired differences, sample standard deviations and effect sizes accompany complete five-seed comparisons. Appendix~\ref{app:protocols} defines all three protocols and their distinct comparator sets.

\subsection{Why a fixed false-alarm budget}
False alarms affect the usefulness of detection when attacks are rare: the base rate enters alarm precision, so high detection alone is insufficient \cite{axelsson2000base}. Operational errors also have substantial costs \cite{sommer2010outside}. Arp et al.'s P7 cautions against unsuitable performance measures; their P8 recommends precision/recall analysis under realistic prevalence, not an exclusive fixed-FPR metric \cite{arp2022dos}. We therefore retain precision, recall and the default-decision view alongside budgeted evaluation. Published precedents include EMBER's malware detection rates below 1\% and 0.1\% FPR \cite{anderson2018ember}, and Kitsune's network-anomaly TPR/FNR at FPR $0$ and $0.001$ \cite{mirsky2018kitsune}. Neither establishes 1\% as a universal class-incremental NIDS standard.

Our empirical motivation is that default decisions incur different alarm burdens: final UNSW FPR averages 25.07\% for R3-L and 1.26\% for AR-Stateless over seeds 10--14. Their default accuracies remain valid measurements of their own decision policies, but do not compare detection at a matched false-alarm budget. After each task calibration uses the permanent, optimization-excluded benign pool and a common empirical false-alarm cap. The decision maps are not identical: AR-Stateless and neural gates use a strict score threshold; the multiclass Replay-BS/Freq/WACB path selects a feasible logit-prior adjustment by bracketing and bisection. The historical permanent pool was also available to allocation validation, so it is not a fresh independent calibration sample. This is a Neyman--Pearson-inspired operating constraint, not an implementation of Tong et al.'s umbrella algorithm or a finite-sample population-error guarantee \cite{tong2018np}. Indeed, that work distinguishes empirical type-I error control from high-probability population control. Across all stages and seeds 10--14, AR-Stateless's measured test FPR at the 1\% target ranges from 0.75--1.15\% on UNSW, 0.94--1.06\% on CIC-2017, and 0.88--1.04\% on CSE-2018. Historical Section 46 NSL-KDD external-baseline records (Replay, Freq, WACB, DER++ and Joint; seeds 1--4/42; four stages each) span 0.75--7.76\% at the 1\% target, independently recomputed from 100 confusion matrices. This demonstrates calibration shift, not a universal 3--8\% range or a result for the new confirmation seeds.

The UNSW pool contains 6,825 benign rows and CIC-2017 contains 181,176. Under a strict empirical $\leq\alpha$ cap, UNSW permits at most $\lfloor6825\alpha\rfloor$ false alarms: 68 at 1\%, but only 6 at 0.1\% (not 7). This illustrates resolution and tail-sample limitations, not operational acceptability of 1\%. The 2\% and 5\% points provide sensitivity checks. Historically, work-order Section 27 used 2\% as primary; Section 48 moved the low-FPR claim to 1\%; Section 165 registered R1--R5 before the seed-10--14 confirmation runs. This sequence does not make the earlier development evidence unexposed. ER-Bal-TabT (official-code-based adaptation), Freq, WACB, Replay-BS, R3-L and neural AR are evaluated under the common budget protocol while retaining their stated data-access rules. The Section 178 baselines join this comparison only when completed and verified. Budgeted evaluation is an evaluation choice, not a proposed mechanism or contribution.

\subsection{Design-space study and negative variants}
The complete 48-configuration screen used seeds 1--2, with three selected configurations evaluated on seeds 10--14. None met K1 on either dataset; no configuration was confirmed, and no further method round follows. Tables~\ref{tab:design-space-screen}--\ref{tab:design-space-confirm} preserve every screening configuration and the confirmation-stage comparators. T1/T2 were registered but not run (time), before scoring; they are not failed model results.

At the 1\% calibration target, the leading selected configuration reaches final BAcc 51.2\% on UNSW and 90.0\% on CIC, versus ER-Bal-TabT's 56.3\% and 92.7\%. Its signed forgetting is 8.8 versus 25.0 points on UNSW, but 2.9 versus 2.7 on CIC: the forgetting advantage is not universal. AR-Joint is also retained as a negative variant (45.7\% and 79.3\% final BAcc). K2 passing means failure to detect significant worsening under its original rule, not demonstrated non-inferiority or equivalence. Among the selected UNSW configurations, forgetting ranges from about 8.1 to 11.6 points, so their similarity in final accuracy must not be generalized to every endpoint.

The candidate mechanism is asymmetric retention: refresh the separate benign--attack gate without retention regularization, while retaining old-attack knowledge in an attack-only family learner. DER-style replay, weight alignment and calibration are existing methods. The negative confirmation outcome neither establishes substantive novelty nor changes the locked research questions.
\subsection{A useful result without a universal winner}
The strongest supported observation is dataset dependent: independently calibrated benign-refresh models substantially improve the selected external-baseline comparison on UNSW-NB15, whereas NSL-KDD does not improve the relevant registered endpoints. The evidence does not support a blanket superiority claim. In particular, favorable binary ADR can coexist with poor family discrimination: detected attacks assigned to the wrong family count as detected in ADR but not as correct in the multiclass metrics.

The work also separates a design hypothesis from its identification. TS-mem shows the outcome of restricting benign refresh within a two-model architecture. The completed Replay-BS comparison strengthens the UNSW-NB15 evidence beyond an explanation based solely on additional benign exposure. Its NSL-KDD result does not support the same benefit, and its single network still receives less total model training than the gate-plus-family configuration. The supported claim is therefore an exposure-matched, dataset-dependent configuration benefit, not a compute-isolated causal estimate of decomposition.

The TS-mem--AA-noAttMem contrast changes benign exposure, realized gate updates and temporary storage together. The attack-memory contrast isolates a different allocation decision within that design, but its nonsignificant detection difference does not establish zero benefit or dispensability. Similarly, the gate/family decomposition partitions observed errors; it cannot by itself attribute forgetting to weight interference, because the operating threshold also changes between checkpoints.

\subsection{Calibration and deployment assumptions}
Clean labeled benign data are a substantial resource. They may be contaminated, costly to curate, or distributionally unrepresentative of future traffic. Refresh from a fixed training pool is only a benchmark surrogate for continuous benign availability. It does not validate resilience to poisoned benign pools, temporal drift, network migration, or delayed labels. NSL-KDD's calibration-to-test shift illustrates why a nominal calibration target must not be equated with realized deployment FPR.

The test-Normal sensitivity analysis deliberately uses test labels and is not a deployable calibration rule or a replacement for the primary result. Threshold tightening exchanges false alarms for missed attacks, and same-population calibration does not establish out-of-sample reliability. Post-hoc mean curves at a common threshold and averages of per-seed interpolated ADR at a fixed FPR are different summaries; neither changes the holdout-calibrated protocol decisions.

\subsection{Scope of statistical and architectural claims}
The benchmark uses few paired seeds, fixed task orders, a finite known label universe, prepared tabular features, and a shared training implementation. These choices support controlled within-protocol comparisons but do not cover arbitrary task order, unseen network environments, or open-world label discovery. A matched schedule is not matched total computation or exposure. The fixed-capacity extension uses both UNSW and NSL for validation selection, so even its later five-seed results on these datasets are developmental, not blind confirmation. CSE-CIC-IDS2018 was withheld from this selection and assessed only after complete guarded release; it fails the registered conjunction. Heterogeneous local/HPC execution limits cost comparison, and withholding results does not establish independent replication. Joint remains an offline reference whose available information differs from incremental methods.

All reported paired tests are unadjusted. Selection of a comparator from the highest or lowest observed whole-group mean and the small seed count limit inferential strength. A nonsignificant harm test is not an equivalence or noninferiority result. KD was not selected as the primary version by validation: its UNSW-NB15 reassessment is exploratory, and its NSL-KDD outcomes were already available before direct authorization. The first two NSL seeds also predate the reassessment document. We therefore do not describe NSL as blind confirmation or infer whether those available results had been inspected.

The initial manuscript window left the CIC-IDS-2017 KD group incomplete. A supplemental retention job reached the scheduler's \texttt{DEADLINE} state without allocation or execution; that job produced no scientific outcome. The subsequent deadline-free continuation completed all five KD and all five original AA-CIL trajectories. The later supplemental Replay-BS seed 42 closes its four-seed gap. Only the affected fixed-KD final-BAcc paired comparison was updated; other historical tests remain unchanged. Earlier operating-point tables and their associated interpretation retain their original four-seed Replay-BS snapshot, not a current missingness claim. Missing trajectories were missing evidence rather than negative model results.

The targeted literature review does not establish first-of-its-kind status. In particular, external benign data and attack-only old memory already appear in neighboring adaptation work. Our restricted-refresh ablation is not an implementation of that literature. Full-text unavailability for Wang and Cao further limits precise differentiation. A novel substantive contribution would require verified distinction and an identifiable benefit beyond combining a gate, replay, and threshold calibration; neither architecture naming nor an external-baseline gain alone meets that standard.

\subsection{Relation to the broader research questions}
This manuscript addresses predictive retention, class-wise safety, and cost under class-incremental updates. It does not measure silent explanation drift across checkpoints, and it does not evaluate non-suppressive explanation governance. Those explanatory and governance questions remain unanswered by these experiments. Predictive gains alone cannot establish stable explanations or the safety of an explanation-governance mechanism.

The selected AR-Stateless candidate improves final and mean incremental balanced accuracy over ER-Bal-TabT on UNSW and CIC-2017 at the registered 1\% calibration target. Class-incremental NIDS must balance false alarms, attack detection and retention as new families arrive. The earlier neural comparisons support a trade-off study: retention components can reduce forgetting while impairing learning of new classes. \subsection{Class-incremental retention and forgetting}
Class-incremental performance is evaluated before the false-alarm--detection comparison. For every saved checkpoint $t=1,\ldots,T$, the operating threshold is determined anew from the same permanent Normal calibration pool at $\alpha=2\%$, using that checkpoint's scores. The final threshold is not retrospectively applied to earlier models. For a seen class $c$, let $r_{c,t}$ denote its recall under this operating point and let $\mathcal C_t$ be the seen-class set. We report
\begin{align}
 B_t&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal C_t|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C_t}r_{c,t}, &
 \overline B_{\mathrm{inc}}&=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}B_t,
\end{align}
together with final balanced accuracy $B_T$. Mean incremental balanced accuracy weights stages equally, rather than pooling their examples or reporting micro accuracy.

Let $\tau_c$ be the introduction stage of class $c$ and $\mathcal O=\{c:\tau_c<T\}$. Signed final forgetting is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F=\frac{1}{|\mathcal O|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal O}
 \left(\max_{\tau_c\leq t<T}r_{c,t}-r_{c,T}\right).
\end{equation}
The historical maximum excludes the final stage; negative values therefore retain final improvement and are not clipped to zero. The primary definition includes Normal whenever it belongs to $\mathcal O$; an attack-only version excludes Normal from this same set. Classes introduced in the final task have no subsequent update and are not assigned a forgetting value. No rare-class support filter is applied to these Class-IL summaries: the support filter belongs only to the separate attack-family safety guard. We report per-seed values and their mean and sample standard deviation, with forgetting in percentage points. Low forgetting alone is not a ranking of useful retention: a class that was never learned well has little recall to lose. It must be read together with final and mean incremental balanced accuracy for every method. Incomplete trajectories or seed groups remain explicitly incomplete.
